Robots That Ask For Help: Uncertainty Alignment for Large Language Model Planners

CoRL 2023 Princeton University & Google DeepMind

▼

Authors

A. Ren, et al.

Conference

CoRL (PMLR 229)

Year

2023

DOI

10.48550/arXiv.2307.01928

Fig 1: Human-robot collaboration, relevant to robots seeking assistance when uncertain.

Objective

The main goal is to address the problem of an LLM-based robot planner making mistakes when faced with uncertainty. The researchers aimed to create a system where the robot knows when it is unsure and asks a human for help instead of guessing and failing.

Methodology (KnowNo Framework)

The researchers developed the "KnowNo" framework. It works in three steps: (1) The LLM planner writes down possible next steps as multiple-choice options. (2) A statistical method checks which options are impossible. (3) If more than one option remains, the robot pauses and asks the human to pick the correct one.

1. Instruction
"Put bowl in microwave"
→
2. LLM Planner
Lists options A-D
→
3. Uncertainty Check
Removes impossible options
→
4. Outcome
Ask human if >1 option left

Results

The framework was tested on a real kitchen robot. The results showed a significant improvement in task success rate and a reduction in errors.

Task Success on a Real Kitchen Robot
KnowNo (asks 67% of time)76% Success
Simple Set (asks 81% of time)75% Success
Never asks51% Success

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

The paper concludes that asking for help when unsure makes robots much more reliable. For RoboPet AI, if a user gives a confusing command, our robot should not guess. Instead, it will use this method to ask for clarification, ensuring a better user experience.

Understanding Large-Language Model (LLM)-powered Human-Robot Interaction

HRI 2024 ACM/IEEE International Conference

▼

Authors

A. Chakma, et al.

Conference

ACM/IEEE HRI

Year

2024

DOI

10.1145/3610977.3634966

Fig 1: A social robot engaging in a conversation, demonstrating LLM-powered HRI.

Objective

This paper investigates the capabilities and limitations of using LLMs (like GPT) to power human-robot interaction. The goal is to understand how well these AI models handle real-world conversations with users and where they fail.

Methodology & Key Findings

The researchers conducted user studies where participants interacted with a robot powered by an LLM. They analyzed the conversations, tracking how well the robot understood context, how often it made errors, and how users perceived the interaction.

Summary of Capabilities vs. Limitations
AspectCapability (Pros)Limitation (Cons)
ConversationNatural, human-like responsesCan be overly verbose or irrelevant
ContextGood at short-term memoryLoses track over long conversations
AccuracyKnowledgeable on many topicsHallucinates (makes up facts)

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

The paper concludes that while LLMs are powerful, they must be carefully integrated. For RoboPet AI, this paper guides our software architecture. We will implement context management to remember past interactions and ensure our robot does not give false information during storytelling.

Ethical Considerations in Socially Assistive Robotics

ACM FAccT 2021

▼

Authors

K. Anderson, et al.

Conference

ACM FAccT

Year

2021

DOI

10.1145/3442188.3445936

Fig 1: Ethical implications of AI and robotics in society.

Objective

To analyze the ethical implications of deploying social robots for vulnerable populations, such as the elderly and children.

Methodology

The authors conducted a qualitative analysis of existing literature and case studies. They identified key ethical risks such as loss of privacy, emotional manipulation, and deception.

Key Ethical Principles Identified
1

Privacy

Data must be securely stored and not shared without consent.

2

Transparency

The robot should not pretend to be human.

3

Non-maleficence

Avoid causing emotional harm or unhealthy dependency.

Results

The paper highlights the need for strict privacy protections, transparent robot behavior (so users know it's a robot), and designing systems that avoid manipulating the user's emotions.

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

This paper serves as a crucial guide for RoboPet AI. It reminds us that as we build a companion robot, we must prioritize user privacy, ensure our robot does not deceive the user, and design it to be a healthy companion rather than an unhealthy dependency.

Objective

To review the technical challenges of implementing Voice User Interfaces (VUI) in social robots, especially in noisy environments.

The researchers reviewed various microphone technologies and signal processing algorithms. They tested how well different setups could isolate a human voice from background noise like music or other conversations.

Directional Mic + Noise Canceling85% Accuracy

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

This paper provides technical guidance for RoboPet AI. It emphasizes that for our robot to hear users properly in a home or classroom, we must invest in good quality directional microphones and noise-canceling software.

▼

Authors

M. H. et al.

Conference

IEEE ICRA

Year

2023

DOI

10.1109/ICRA48891.2023.10160942

Fig 1: Software architecture and code structure for social robots.

Objective

To propose a modular software architecture that makes it easier to develop, maintain, and upgrade social robot applications.

Methodology

The authors designed a layered architecture with clear interfaces. They separated the software into three main parts: Perception (sensors), Decision (AI and planning), and Action (motors and speech). They then implemented this architecture on a test robot.

Modular Software Architecture
ModuleFunctionComponent
PerceptionSense environmentCameras, Mic, Sensors
DecisionThink and planAI, LLM, Logic
ActionInteractMotors, Speaker

Results

The proposed architecture enabled rapid development and easy integration of new capabilities. It improved code reusability and reduced development time. Adding a new sensor or behavior required minimal changes to the core system.

Development Efficiency Improvements
Code Reusability+70%
Reduction in Development Time-40%
Ease of Adding New FeaturesHigh

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

This paper directly guides the software architecture of RoboPet AI. By adopting this modular approach, our team (especially Rafi and Anik) can work on different parts of the software simultaneously without breaking the whole system.
