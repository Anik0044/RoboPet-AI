▼

Authors

M. Ali, et al.

Conference

ACM/IEEE HRI

Year

2026

Topic

Tactile Sensing

Fig 1: Self-capacitive tactile sensor array with flexible circuits.

Objective

To design a simple, low-cost, and scalable tactile sensing system that allows a companion robot to detect and distinguish different types of touch.

Methodology

The sensor uses a single conductive fabric layer. A 100-point sensor array was fabricated on a flexible printed circuit. A decision-tree classifier on an FPGA was used to process touch data in real-time.

Touch Classification Types

Hit97%

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

For RoboPet AI, this paper provides a concrete method for building a full-body touch sensor. It solves the problem of how our robot can distinguish between a gentle pet and a rough tap, enabling emotionally appropriate responses.

▼

Authors

Y. Zhang, et al.

Journal

IEEE Sensors

Year

2024

Topic

Flexible Sensors

Fig 1: Flexible electronic skin sensors for robotic applications.

Objective

To review the latest advancements in flexible tactile sensors that can be used to create "electronic skin" for social robots.

Methodology

The authors analyzed various sensor technologies including capacitive, piezoresistive, and piezoelectric sensors. They compared their flexibility, sensitivity, and cost.

Comparison of Tactile Sensor Technologies
TypeSensitivityFlexibilityCost
CapacitiveHighMediumMedium
PiezoresistiveMediumHighLow
PiezoelectricHighLowHigh

Results

The review found that piezoresistive sensors offer the best balance of flexibility and cost, making them ideal for robotic pets. Capacitive sensors offer higher sensitivity but are more expensive.

Overall Suitability for Robotic Pets
Piezoresistive (Best Balance)85%
Capacitive (High Sensitivity)70%
Piezoelectric (High Cost)50%

Conclusion & Relevance to RoboPet AI

This review guides the selection of tactile sensors for RoboPet AI. Based on this, we will use piezoresistive sensors for our robot's body to achieve a good balance of sensitivity and cost.
