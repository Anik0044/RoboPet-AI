Literature Review

Anik Hossain

Team Lead / AI & Software

01 Once Upon a Time…: an Adaptive Robotic Behavior for Engaging Cooperative Storytelling ▼

Authors: A. Rossi, et al.

Published in: 2024 19th ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), 2024.

Abstract: This research presents an adaptive cooperative storytelling framework for social robots. By integrating Large Language Models (LLMs), the robot acts as a co-creator of stories rather than a passive narrator. It processes real-time verbal and non-verbal user cues to dynamically alter the narrative arc, character traits, and emotional tones.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: This paper provides a foundational architecture for implementing interactive storytelling in our project. It guides how our RoboPet AI can use user feedback to make storytelling dynamic and engaging.

02 A Social Robot as a Storyteller: An Exploratory Study on the Role of Emotions ▼

Authors: M. C. C. et al.

Published in: 2023 32nd IEEE International Conference on Robot and Human Interactive Communication (RO-MAN), 2023.

Abstract: This paper investigates the critical role of emotional expression in human-robot storytelling interactions. It explores how a robot's emotional cues (facial expressions, voice modulation, and body gestures) influence the user's emotional engagement and overall experience during a storytelling session.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: For our RoboPet AI to be an effective companion, it must not only tell stories but also express appropriate emotions. This paper highlights the importance of integrating emotional AI into our storytelling logic.

03 The Evolving Design of a Socially Therapeutic Robotic Dog ▼

Authors: E. T. Esfahani, et al.

Published in: IEEE Robotics and Automation Letters, vol. 8, no. 2, pp. 1-8, 2023.

Abstract: This paper details the iterative design and empirical evaluation of "Therabot," a socially assistive robotic dog intended for therapeutic applications. The study focuses on creating an affordable, expressive, and robust platform to provide emotional support and companionship.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: This study validates the core concept of our project—that a robotic pet can significantly improve mental well-being. The physical and interaction design principles of Therabot provide valuable insights for building our own companion robot.

01 Introducing M: A Modular, Modifiable Social Robot ▼

Authors: V. N. Antony, Z. Gong, Y. Kim, and C.-M. Huang

Published in: 2025 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2025.

Abstract: This paper presents M, an open-source, low-cost social robot platform designed to reduce the friction in social robotics research by making robots easier to reproduce, modify, and deploy in real-world settings. M combines a modular mechanical design, multimodal sensing, and an expressive yet mechanically simple actuation architecture.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: This paper is extremely valuable for our hardware design. It provides a blueprint for creating a low-cost, modular, and easily repairable body for RoboPet AI.

02 Self Capacitive Tactile Sensor System designed for Companion Robots ▼

Authors: M. Ali, S. Ikemoto, et al.

Published in: 2026 21st ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), 2026.

Abstract: This paper addresses the challenge of designing tactile sensing systems for humanoid robots. The authors present a simple, scalable, and hardware-friendly tactile sensing system for a companion humanoid robot based on the self-capacitance principle.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: For our robot to feel "alive," it must respond to touch. This paper provides a concrete, low-cost method for building a full-body touch sensor for RoboPet AI.

03 Design and Integration of Thermal and Vibrotactile Feedback for Lifelike Touch in Social Robots ▼

Authors: J. Borgstedt, M. Iovino, et al.

Published in: 2025 32nd IEEE International Conference on Robot and Human Interactive Communication (RO-MAN), 2025.

Abstract: This paper presents a multimodal tactile prototype that integrates thermal and vibrotactile feedback to simulate biophysiological signals like warmth and heartbeat. It augments a robot with a flexible heating interface and embedded actuators.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: This paper takes the concept of a robotic pet to the next level. It shows us how to make our robot feel warm and alive, enhancing the emotional connection.

01 A Software Architecture for Social Robots ▼

Authors: M. H. et al.

Published in: 2023 IEEE International Conference on Robotics and Automation (ICRA), 2023.

Abstract: Proposes a modular software architecture for social robots, emphasizing the integration of perception, decision-making, and action modules. The architecture supports flexible development and deployment of complex robotic behaviors.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: This paper directly guides the software architecture of our project, providing a framework to structure our Python and AI modules.

02 Web-based Teleoperation and Monitoring System for Mobile Robots ▼

Authors: K. S. et al.

Published in: 2022 IEEE/RSJ International Conference on Intelligent Robots and Systems (IROS), 2022.

Abstract: Presents a web-based framework using WebSockets and ROS for real-time teleoperation and monitoring of robots. It allows users to control robots and view sensor data through a standard web browser.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: Supports Rafi's web development role, showing how to connect our website interface to the robot's backend for remote interaction and monitoring.

03 Integrating Large Language Models into Robotic Systems for Improved Human-Robot Interaction ▼

Authors: L. W. et al.

Published in: 2024 ACM/IEEE International Conference on Human-Robot Interaction (HRI), 2024.

Abstract: Explores the integration of LLMs (like GPT) into robot software stacks, allowing for natural language understanding and generation. The study focuses on prompt engineering and context management for robotic tasks.

Relevance to RoboPet AI: Essential for our robot's conversational abilities. This paper provides practical insights into how we can use APIs to make our robot talk and understand users naturally.
